# Moonbase Commander

**Team Cupcoffee** | NASA Space Apps Challenge | Challenge: Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

Play it in your browser: **https://shivamdwivedicse.github.io/Moonbase-commander/**

## What it is

Moonbase Commander is a browser game for students aged about 10 to 14. You are the commander of a Moon outpost with four astronauts, and you have to keep them alive for 30 days.

Every fix costs something somewhere else, just like on a real mission:

- Piling moon soil (regolith) on the habitat cuts radiation, but uses crew shifts and power.
- Solar panels are cheap, but make nothing during the long lunar night. A fission power unit works day and night, but takes three shifts to build and adds a little radiation.
- Potatoes give more food than salad greens, but need more power.
- Power saver mode saves electricity, but slows the garden and lowers morale.
- Moonwalks lift morale, but expose the crew to radiation outside.

## How to play

1. Pick a landing site: Peak of eternal light, Crater rim near ice, or Sunny plains. Each has a different pattern of sunny and dark days.
2. Pick Cadet (easier) or Commander.
3. Each day your crew has a few work shifts. Pick a build from the bottom bar and press **Do it**.
4. Watch the top bar: battery, oxygen, food, radiation and morale. The strip under it shows sunny and dark days, so you can plan ahead.
5. Press **End day**. Events can happen, like solar flares, dust on the panels or a leak.
6. If battery, oxygen, food or morale run out, or the radiation budget is used up, the mission ends. Reach day 30 to win and earn stars.
7. The **Mission Report** shows what happened, what real engineers do about it, and real space science facts. Press the report button at any time to see one.

## NASA data and resources used

- **NASA DONKI** (Space Weather Database Of Notifications, Knowledge, Information), through the NASA Open APIs: https://api.nasa.gov
  The game fetches real solar flares from the last 30 days and uses them as in-game events. If the browser cannot reach NASA, the game falls back to sample flares and labels them "sample event".
- **NASA public information** (NASA Artemis, Human Research Program, ISS, CHAPEA) for the science facts shown in the Mission Report. Facts are simplified for young learners.

## Other data and resources

- Chang'e-4 lunar surface radiation measurement (Science Advances, 2020), used to set the rough daily radiation level on the Moon.
- Fredoka and Nunito fonts from Google Fonts (SIL Open Font License): https://fonts.google.com

## Important note about the numbers

Game numbers (power, oxygen, food, radiation budget and so on) are **simplified so the game is fun and readable**. They are not real engineering values. All of them live in one `CFG` block near the top of the script in `index.html`.
